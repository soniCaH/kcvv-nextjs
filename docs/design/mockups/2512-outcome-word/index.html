Where the outcome word goes

Five surfaces draw the win/loss band. Only some print a word beside it. This page shows every one of them as it renders today, and exactly what changes. The answer turned out to be much smaller than the question: one surface changes its word, one loses a duplicate band, and the draw gets a colour everywhere. Decision record for #2512, under map #2506.

The word appears only where nothing else in the row assigns the score. Two clubs either side of it, or a Thuis/Uit tag beside it, and the row already names who won — the word is then noise.

Half of this was already written — TeamAgendaRow.tsx:287–301 (#2404) argues the two-club case. #2512 added the venue-tag case and made it one rule for all five surfaces.

First, the band

Q1 = B made the tint a complete three-case system, so the draw needs a colour. Q5 = A picks --color-ink-muted mixed 34% into cream — the only neutral that matches the other two bands in strength. Ratios are against cream #f5f1e6; #2513 measured 1.62 and 1.69 reading fine outdoors on a phone.

Besloten

Slot word → outcome word, green → ink. The column stays w-14, so the opponent name loses nothing.

15 sepGelijk SKB SK Berlaar-Heikant 2 – 2

“Gelijk”, not “Gelijkspel”. The column is sized for “Volgende” (8 characters). “Gelijkspel” is 10 and would have forced w-16, taking 8px off a truncated opponent name — the exact trade #2388 fought. “Gelijk” is 6 and fits, so the width question never arises.

Why the word is redundant here, truncation and all. The home club is always printed first, and <MatchVenueTag> says which side KCVV is on. Read Uit and the second number is ours — no matter how hard the names clip. This row is not colour-only, so the rule from the top of the page does not reach it.
