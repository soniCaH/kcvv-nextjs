Design decision pass · D8 · §5C · D6

A printed contents page for the whole site?

One route, no images, listing everything: every team with its division, every player with their team and number, every article with its date, every event, every club page. Display serif section heads, mono entries, leader dots, three columns collapsing to one.

Half of that list cannot be built, and the sheet already said so.

§5C contradicts A17's neighbour — A7 — and the data behind it

A7 — No /spelers index. Players stay reachable via team pages. An index of ~300 players U6–U21 has privacy implications (→ C12) and no demonstrated user need. Treat the absence as deliberate.

§5C proposes exactly that index, as a section of a bigger one. And C12 measured why it could not be built even if it were wanted: 294 player documents, 0 with a slug — so there are no pages to link to — and 0 with a jersey number, so the "with their team and number" column would be empty on every row.

So the players section is impossible twice over: rejected on privacy, and unbuildable on data. What is left is teams, articles, events and club pages.

B — the index that can actually exist

Everything derived from the CMS or the BFF, nothing authored. Counts are the real production figures.

Spelers — cannot be built

294 spelers · 0 met een slug (geen pagina om naar te linken) · 0 met een rugnummer. En A7 heeft een spelersindex al afgewezen.

About 240 rows. The leader dots are §4.4's S5, which is asked separately in D10 — they are used here because §5C specifies them, not because they are decided.

What already does this job

/zoeken exists and is backed by a vector index — it answers "where is the thing I am looking for" better than a list of 240 rows a reader has to scan.

The nav is flat and each destination indexes its own children — the four dropdowns were deleted deliberately in #2409/#2415. /ploegen lists the teams, /nieuws lists the articles, /evenementen lists the events. An index is a fifth listing of things already listed four times.

§5C's own warning applies to us specifically: every entry must be derived, never authored — and llms.txt has already drifted exactly this way once, shipping /club/organigram long after the route was removed.

The honest case in favour: none of those surfaces shows the whole site at once, and a contents page is the most fanzine-native object there is. It is a gesture, and gestures are allowed — the question is whether this one earns a route.

A cheaper shape, if the gesture is what appeals. The same device could be a section rather than a route — a contents block in the footer, or at the foot of /zoeken, listing the four groups with counts and linking to the existing indexes. It keeps the printed-contents gesture, costs no new route, and cannot drift because it lists four things instead of 240.
