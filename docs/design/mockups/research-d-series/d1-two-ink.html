How far does the two-ink fiction reach?

The proposal: commit to the story that this site is printed in exactly two inks — ink black and jersey green, on cream stock — and then let the registration be imperfect on purpose. Where green crosses black in multiply, you get a third darker value for free, which is what actually happens on paper.

Everything below is rendered live in the real tokens and the real typefaces. The plate offset is 2px everywhere on this page; the offset itself is the next question, not this one.

Read this first — three things the research doc assumed wrong

These change what is actually on the table, so they belong before the choice rather than after it.

1 — There is no single crest

kcvv-crest-retro.png is a monochrome retro mark (footer, 80–96px). It plates cleanly — the green layer is a mask of its alpha, which is what you see in options C and D.

kcvv-logo.png is the full-colour club crest, and it is the one in the header and the match strip. A colour mark cannot carry a second plate; masking it gives a green halo, not registration. So "the crest gets a plate" is only ever true of the retro crest.

2 — 1.5px is not available

§5A proposes 1.5px and then flags the problem itself: on a 1× display a half pixel lands between device pixels and renders soft. Soft edges are the one thing this design language forbids outright.

So the real ladder is 1 / 2 / 3px, whole pixels only. No min-resolution branch — that would ship two different designs to two different people.

3 — #2598 constrains where this can be tested

The homepage overflow's widest offender is a chain ending in SPAN.font-display.text-cream.text-2xl — a display span in an ink band, constant at 402px across every viewport.

A text plate is position: absolute and adds no layout width, so it is overflow-neutral by construction. But it must not be prototyped on the homepage headline until #2598's root cause is isolated, or the two will be confused.

What this decision unlocks or kills downstream. T4 (ink bleed on the seam edge) only makes sense once the seam carries two plates — it is a refinement of B, not an independent idea. §5B's match-day register lists "the crest gets its misregistration plate, today only" as one of five switches; if the answer here is A, that switch is gone and §5B loses a fifth of its content. T5 (photocopy degradation on a decorative crest) is a different technique on the same asset and would compete with the plate rather than combine with it.
