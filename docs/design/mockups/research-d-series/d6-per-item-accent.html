Design decision pass · D6 · C5

One colour per team — system, or ornament?

§4.3 proposes a saturated tone per card, drawn from a closed list, on the kicker and nothing else. It flags its own risk: it "risks looking like a category-colour system, which is product UI".

The sharper version of that risk is arithmetic.

26 teams, at most 4 tones

Production holds 26 team documents. §4.3 caps the set at four "or it is product UI". Four tones across twenty-six teams means the colour repeats every fourth card — so it cannot identify a team, and a reader who tries to learn it will fail.

That is the whole decision: a colour that varies without meaning anything is ornament wearing the clothes of a system, which is worse than either. The only way out is a set that maps to something real — and we have one, because the youth department is already divided into Bovenbouw / Middenbouw / Onderbouw, which with Senioren is exactly four groups.

Colour now carries information — and immediately duplicates the label. Every card already says "Bovenbouw", and the page already groups by it. Per the project rule, you label what the layout hides; here the layout hides nothing, so the colour is a second encoding of something already stated twice.

The honest summary. B is ornament pretending to be a system. C is a real system that encodes what the words and the grouping already say, and spends two semantic tones (alert, warning) plus green on a taxonomy. A is what ships, and its weakness is real but small: /ploegen is a long undifferentiated grid. If that is the actual complaint, the fix is more likely to be grouping and rhythm than colour — the group bars in C do more work than the colours in C do.
