Ink on the photograph, not a filter over it

Our photos carry --filter-photo-newsprint — a 6% sepia warm shift. T2 proposes a real overprint instead: the green plate sits on the image, shadows take the ink, highlights stay paper, and the photo stays in colour.

Real club photographs below, so skin tones are actually tested.

T2's stated mechanism does the opposite of its stated intent

§4.1 says background-blend-mode: multiply against a jersey-deep→cream ramp, "so shadows go green and highlights stay paper". Multiply cannot do that. Multiply is a × b: a white highlight multiplied by green becomes green. Multiply darkens everything, and it darkens the highlights hardest in relative terms. That is column D.

Its own cited source has the right operator. The note beside it says Stripe Press uses mix-blend-mode: lighten, which is the shadow-weighted operator. Stated precisely, because the loose version misleads: lighten takes the per-channel maximum against the plate. It does not classify whole pixels. A pixel keeps every channel already above the plate and has only its lower channels raised — so a pixel brighter than the plate in all three channels passes through untouched, and a near-neutral dark one lands on the plate.

The consequence that matters, and the limit of the claim. A saturated dark colour has some channels clamped and others not, which shifts its hue rather than preserving it: against #133d28 a dark red kit #6b1010 becomes #6b3d28 (red toward brown) and a deep navy #101a3a becomes #133d3a (navy toward teal). So "highlights keep their colour" holds for anything above the plate but not for dark saturated kit — worth knowing, since dark kit is one of the things this technique is aimed at.

What the decision actually rests on is unaffected. On the skin-tone test both mid and shadowed skin (#c98a6b, #8a5f47) sit above the plate in all three channels and pass through with no channel clamped at all. Faces stay recognisable. That is columns B and C.

Whatever wins is opt-in per figure, not global. T2 proposes it as data-print="overprint" alongside the existing warm tint, which matches how data-tint="none" already works on TapedFigure — so the mechanism has a precedent and needs no new pattern. The cost is one pseudo-element with a blend mode, which promotes a compositing layer per figure: fine on an article hero, worth watching on a grid of news cards.
