One band, or two?

You are right, and it is worth seeing rather than arguing. MatchStripSlot is mounted in the (landing) layout — it is already a band directly under the navigation, on the homepage and every section index — and it is a locked spec (phase-3-c-header-and-matchstrip/matchstrip-locked.md).

So §5B's wire strip is not a new idea in an empty slot. It is a second band on top of a band that already carries the next fixture.

What the shipped strip already knows — and the one thing it does not

MatchStrip renders the last result and the next fixture, with crests, venue glyph, score and date. It has everything the wire strip was going to say.

The single gap: it has no concept of "today". There is no isToday, no Vandaag, nowhere in MatchStrip, dates.ts or match-display.ts. A fixture eight days away and a fixture in two hours render identically.

What it costs

One isToday derivation, computed in Europe/Brussels — PSD match dates are Belgian wall-clock, so a naive UTC comparison flips the label at the wrong hour. Plus a variant on a locked component, which means a checkpoint against matchstrip-locked.md rather than a free edit.

Day-granular, so revalidate = 900 is harmless: the worst case is the label appearing 15 minutes after midnight.

Three chrome elements saying one thing

The wire strip, the marquee and the strip's "Volgende" row all name the same fixture. On a 390px phone that is most of the first screen spent before any content — and the primary scene for this site is a phone outdoors.

The marquee is separable. It could sit on B as well — one band plus a scrolling line — or nowhere. It is the only element in §5B with no informational job at all: everything it says is already in the band above it, in a form you can read without waiting for it to scroll past. That is either the point of it or the case against it.
