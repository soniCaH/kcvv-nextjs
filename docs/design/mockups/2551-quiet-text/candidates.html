C · The dark side already has a rule — the ticket missed it

"Cream on a jersey-deep surface is cream, never a fraction of it. text-cream/90 costs more contrast than a whole shade step of green does, so the fraction is always the worse trade. Scoped to jersey-deep: on jersey-deep-dark (~10.9:1) and ink (17.5:1) cream has contrast to spare and a fraction is harmless." apps/web/DESIGN.md — "The Whole-Cream Rule", ~40 lines below the token list the ticket quoted

So the dark question is not "the site has never named one". It is: the rule is surface-scoped and settled at the jersey-deep boundary, and says nothing about the spread inside the zone it permits. Six alphas live in that permitted zone. Three files already carry the rule's receipts in code:

  • MonoLabel.tsx:37–44 — a docblock recording that text-cream/85 was tried and rejected, citing the rule by name, and prescribing the workaround (wrap the parent, don't bake it into the token). Counted by the ticket as a divergence site.
  • NewsCard.tsx:332–334 — "Full-opacity cream on dark surfaces — opacity-reduced cream (e.g. text-cream/85) trips axe contrast on bg-jersey-deep." Also counted as a divergence site.
  • RaffleCallout.tsx:20 — "Full cream — DESIGN.md 'The Whole-Cream Rule'" on the <p>… while the two <NumberDisplay tone="cream"> beneath it still render text-cream/70 labels. The rule was applied by hand and escaped through the primitive — which is the exception DESIGN.md itself names as still shipping.

The reframe. The ticket asks what the dark answer is, given that none has ever been named. One exists, it is named, and it is scoped by surface rather than by role — which is a different shape from every cream tier this ticket proposes.

The open questions are therefore: (1) how many tiers on cream, given the middle one is invisible; (2) whether the dark answer should stay surface-scoped or be restated as role-scoped to mirror cream; (3) what happens to the six-alpha spread inside the zone the rule already permits.
