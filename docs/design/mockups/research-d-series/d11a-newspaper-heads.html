Design decision pass · D11a · S1, re-researched

Newspapers stacked downward. They had no right edge to hang from.

A headline lived inside its column. The 1912 New York Times Titanic head was a "three-column" head — that was the whole width it got — and it still put four further facts underneath, not beside. Multi-deck headlining exists because the measure was narrow.

So S1's ancestor is not a newspaper. It is a Swiss spec sheet. That does not make it wrong — but if the brief is "oldschool newspaper", there are two other devices with a better claim.

The vocabulary, and what each part did

Deck (also drop head, bank)
A smaller headline below the main one, adding new information rather than restating it. Rule of the craft: typographic contrast (bold main → lighter decks) and size contrast (36pt → 18pt → 14pt). Stacked decks became common during the Mexican-American War, 1846–48.
Cut-off rule
A 1- or 2-point horizontal rule. Used to keep stories apart — and to separate one deck of a headline from the next.
Agate
5.5pt type (or ~7pt relative to a paper's body size) reserved for statistical matter: box scores, standings, transactions, classifieds. The first rudimentary box score ran in the New York Morning News in 1845. Agate lived in its own tabulated block, never attached to the headline.
Dateline
The place a story was filed from, leading the first paragraph: ELEWIJT, 12 april —

The constraint that matters to us: agate is 5.5–7pt. Our 11px Floor Rule forbids it, and text-label-sm at 10px is already recorded as the floor and already spent. A faithful agate block is illegal here — which is exactly why a modern spec-sheet device exists in the first place.

What a 1912 sports page would actually have done here

Both C and D, for different facts. Identity — who, when, which competition — went into the decks, because it is part of naming the story. Statistics — referee, attendance, lineups, scorers with minutes — went into the agate block, set apart and tabulated.

That split maps cleanly onto our data. Decks carry the fields the BFF always provides. The agate block carries the ones `PRODUCT.md` warns are unreliable — venue, competition metadata, is_home — where a missing value is a dash in a table rather than a hole in a headline.
