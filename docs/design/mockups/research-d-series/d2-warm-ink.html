Is the ink neutral, or does it sit on the paper warm?

--color-ink: #0a0a0a is neutral — it is a screen black on a warm paper. Stripe Press runs #201819; most print-referencing sites pick an ink that shares a hue with the stock. The claim is that a warm near-black reads as printed and a neutral one reads as rendered.

This is one token, and it is the widest-blast-radius change in the whole D-series: --color-ink is the glyph colour, the band ground, and the ink every --shadow-paper-* is drawn in.

The adjacency test

A 6-point hue shift is invisible in isolation and obvious side by side, so judge it here first. Same cream, same glyph, four inks touching. The fourth is the opposite direction — included so the axis is legible, not as a candidate.

Legible as a decision. Warm enough to name, and noticeably less dense than today — which softens the 2px borders and every hard shadow at the same time. That softening is the actual risk, not the hue.

What it costs, stated plainly

The blast radius is the argument, not the hue

Every visual regression baseline in the repo moves. This is a site-wide token change, so a scoped local capture cannot cover it — the unscoped guard refuses it, which makes this a bot-driven re-baseline of the entire suite. Nothing else in the D-series has that property.

The seven shadow tokens are drawn in ink. --shadow-paper-sm/md/lift all resolve to var(--color-ink), so warming the ink warms every hard shadow on the site simultaneously. That is arguably the point — a printed shadow is the same ink as the type — but it means the change cannot be scoped to text.

Contrast is not the tie-breaker here. On cream: today 17.5:1, subtle warm 16.8:1, Stripe value 15.4:1. All three are far past any threshold that would decide anything, so this is a readability-by-eye call, which is the project's stated test anyway.

It cannot be done twice. Changing ink once is a re-baseline; changing it, living with it, and changing it back is two. If it is going to be tried, it wants to be tried on a branch with a full capture and looked at on a real phone in daylight before it merges.

Interaction with the rest of the queue. T3 (a real scanned-paper tile behind the page) is the other half of the substrate question and is asked separately — but the two compound: a warm ink on procedural noise is half a move, and a warm ink on real stock is the whole one. If the answer here is A, T3 still stands on its own; if the answer here is B or C, T3 should be decided in the same sitting so the substrate ships as one change rather than two re-baselines.
